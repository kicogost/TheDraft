# The launch playbook

**Two B2B companies launched on LinkedIn this year. Here's what the posts did, and the five days of work that happened before either one existed.**

By Francisco Gost · The Draft · franciscogost.com

---

## Why this exists

I'm a partner at RallyUp, where we write LinkedIn for B2B founders and executives at Airwallex, Letter AI, Anchor Browser and Andel. More than 750 posts so far in 2026.

Launches are the thing people get wrong most predictably, and they get it wrong in the same way every time: they treat the post as the launch. The post is maybe a fifth of it, and it's the fifth that happens last.

What follows is the sequence we actually run, with the numbers from two real launches attached to it. One was a fourth-time founder with a funding announcement. The other had never launched anything on LinkedIn in his life.

If something in here doesn't have a number next to it, I've said so.

## The two launches

### Andel, 25 June 2026

A pharma marketplace launching "Andel for Plans" alongside a $4.5M raise, posted as a video.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Impressions | **181,619** |
| Video views | 64,122 |
| Unique viewers | 61,963 |
| Total watch time | **137 hours** |
| Reactions, comments, shares | 84 / 20 / 9 |
| New followers | 28 |
| Link clicks | 15 |

### LetsGo, 18 August 2026

An AI mentoring marketplace. First launch, text and image, no video.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Impressions | **38,291** |
| **Link clicks** | **554** |
| **Comments** | **343** |
| Reactions | 461 |
| New followers | 36 |
| Profile views | 352 |
| Saves | 27 |

The waitlist filled in under 24 hours, and the campaign produced more than a thousand signups that scored as qualified against the company's own ICP.

Those two tables are different shapes, and that's the first thing worth noticing. Andel bought an enormous amount of attention and almost no direct action; fifteen people clicked. LetsGo bought a fifth of the attention and got 554 clicks plus 343 comments out of it.

Neither one is the better launch. They were built for different jobs, which is the first decision you make and the one most people skip.

---

## 1. Decide what the launch is for

A post can really only do one of these well.

**Awareness** means you want the category to know you exist. Video, broad story, low friction, almost no asks. Andel's launch is this shape, and 137 hours of watch time against fifteen clicks is exactly what that trade looks like.

**Pipeline** means you want qualified people in a list by Friday. Text, a specific offer, two clear actions. That's LetsGo.

**Credibility** is the quiet one: you want people who already know you to take you more seriously. Numbers, proof, named partners. It'll get the fewest impressions of the three and the highest quality.

Pick one before you write, and grade the post against that one afterwards. A launch built for pipeline that gets judged on impressions will always look like a failure, and the reverse is just as true.

---

## 2. Build the amplifier list before you write the post

Nobody copies this step, which is why it still works.

You want roughly 50 close contacts, plus 100 or more people you've met over the years, plus customers and investors. Not everyone you know. People who'd be genuinely pleased for you, because the ask only lands if the answer is obviously yes.

Four columns in a spreadsheet does it: name, channel, timezone, sent. It takes an afternoon and you'll reuse it for every launch after this one.

---

## 3. Run the five day sequence

Launch day is day five. Everything that decides the outcome happens on days one to four.

| Day | Who | Channel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Thursday** | Close contacts, around 50 | LinkedIn DM or WhatsApp, each one followed by a calendar invite for launch hour |
| **Sunday** | Investors and network, timezone one | Email |
| **Monday** | Investors and network, timezone two | Email |
| **Monday** | Nudge back to the first fifty | Same thread as Thursday |
| **Tuesday, launch hour** | Everyone | The link drops at once |

**Send the calendar invite.** It sounds like overkill and it does more for the outcome than anything else on this list, because it turns a vague intention into a notification on the morning it matters.

### The ask is the same three words every time

Like, comment, save.

Save is the one people skip, and it's the strongest of the three, so ask for it by name or you won't get it. Add a line about forwarding to anyone who'd get value, because a forward is worth more than all three.

### What the messages say

The wording matters less than these four things.

**Tell them before the world knows.** That's why the ask works at all; you've given them something first, which is advance notice and the feeling of being inside it.

**Say exactly what you want, with a time on it.** Not "support the launch." A like, a one-line comment and a save, in the first hour.

**Say how long it takes.** Two minutes. People will agree to two minutes.

**Give them a way out.** Something like "no pressure at all if Tuesday's a mess for you" costs you nothing, and it's the reason people answer honestly instead of ghosting you.

For investors, lead with a real update before you ask for anything: where the product is, what early customers did, what you're launching with. The ask goes at the bottom, after the substance.

---

## 4. Build two funnels into the post

Here's the mechanic that made LetsGo work, and it's the most copyable thing in this document.

The post didn't say "sign up at the link." It said: comment below, **and** fill in the form on the site. Two actions, deliberately.

> 554 people clicked the link.
> 343 people commented.

Those are two separate funnels, and most of the qualified signups never came through the link at all.

A comment is free, public, and takes four seconds, so people actually do it; every comment pushes the post further, which puts it in front of more people, who comment. The link is private, higher friction, and only the already-convinced bother. Had the post carried only the link, Moe gets his 554 clicks and nothing underneath them.

The qualifying doesn't happen in the comments, by the way. The form asked for role, age and company, scored against who the product was built for. That's how a thousand signups became a number anyone could trust rather than a vanity count.

---

## 5. Use video when the story carries it

Andel's launch video did 64,122 views and 137 hours of watch time, and produced fifteen link clicks.

Video buys attention, not action, because a viewer isn't sitting in a clicking posture. Use it when the launch is about belief: a category, a vision, a founder's reason for bothering. Use text when the launch is about a thing someone has to do, like join a waitlist before Friday.

Cross-post it to X the same hour. Same asset, second room, no extra work.

---

## 6. The forty eight hours after

A launch post is a list of people who raised their hand in public, and almost nobody works that list.

Pull everyone who commented, reacted or saved. Filter to the ones who match your ICP on role, company and seniority, then message every one of them personally, referencing the specific thing they did. You're aiming for a call, a demo or a sale, not a newsletter signup.

This is the step where a launch turns into revenue instead of a nice day on a chart. The list has a shelf life of about a week and it's the warmest one you'll ever hold.

Then post again on day eight. LetsGo's follow-up was the founder's own story of leaving a job with five kids and no plan B, and it did 27,624 impressions with another 284 link clicks, more than a week after launch. A launch is a window, not a day.

---

## 7. What to measure

Three weeks after his launch, the same founder posted something motivational. It did **72,031 impressions**, nearly double the launch post, and produced seven new followers and no signups. The launch did 38,291 and filled a waitlist in a day.

So impressions aren't the scoreboard, they're just the cheapest thing to count, which is why everyone counts them.

| Measure | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Saves per 1,000 impressions** | The closest thing to free intent data LinkedIn gives you. A like is politeness; a save means someone wants to come back to it |
| **Profile views from the post** | Somebody went looking for you, which is a buying motion |
| **Qualified signups, scored** | Scored against your ICP. Raw signups will flatter you |
| **Conversations opened in 48 hours** | The only one that turns into money |

## What this can't do for you

The honest part, because the rest isn't believable without it.

**It won't work without the list.** Both of these launches had a founder with an existing network who was willing to spend four days asking people personally, one at a time. Skip days one to four and post anyway, and you'll get roughly the reach your account already had.

**Two launches isn't a law.** One pharma marketplace with a funding announcement attached, one AI education product with a waitlist, both B2B, both founder-led, both on LinkedIn. If you sell to consumers, treat everything here as a starting hypothesis rather than a method.

**These numbers are reach and engagement, not attribution.** I can show you 554 clicks and 343 comments. I can't draw you a clean line from an impression to a signed contract, because LinkedIn doesn't give that to anybody, and people who claim it are usually counting something else and hoping you won't ask.

**It doesn't work twice in a row on the same list.** The amplifier ask spends goodwill you've built up over years. Save it for launches.

---

## The one page version

1. Decide whether this launch is for awareness, pipeline or credibility, and grade it on that alone.
2. Build the amplifier list before you write a word. Fifty close, a hundred wider.
3. Message them personally four days out, with a calendar invite for launch hour.
4. Ask for like, comment, save. Save is the one that moves it.
5. Put two actions in the post: a comment gate and a link.
6. Qualify on the form, not in the comments.
7. Video for belief, text for action, and cross-post to X either way.
8. Work the engagement list inside 48 hours.
9. Post again on day eight.
10. Count saves, profile views and conversations.
