# The launch playbook

**How two B2B companies launched on LinkedIn, what the posts actually did, and the five days of work that happened before either post existed.**

By Francisco Gost · The Draft · franciscogost.com

---

## Why this exists

I am a partner at RallyUp. We write LinkedIn for B2B founders and executives at Airwallex, Letter AI, Anchor Browser and Andel. More than 750 posts in 2026.

Launches are the part people get wrong most predictably, because almost everyone treats the post as the launch. The post is maybe a fifth of it.

This is the sequence we actually run, with the numbers from two real launches attached. One of them is a 25 year old founder on his fourth company. The other had never launched anything on LinkedIn before.

Everything in here has a number next to it or it is not in here.

## The two launches

### Andel, 25 June 2026

A pharma marketplace, launching "Andel for Plans" alongside a $4.5M raise. The post was a video.

| Impressions | **181,619** |
| Video views | 64,122 |
| Unique viewers | 61,963 |
| Total watch time | **137 hours** |
| Reactions / comments / shares | 84 / 20 / 9 |
| New followers | 28 |
| Profile views | 96 |

### LetsGo, 18 August 2026

An AI mentoring marketplace. First launch, text and image, no video.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Impressions | **38,291** |
| **Link clicks** | **554** |
| **Comments** | **343** |
| Reactions | 461 |
| New followers | 36 |
| Profile views | 352 |
| Saves | 27 |

The waitlist filled in under 24 hours. The campaign produced more than 1,000 signups that scored as qualified against the company's ICP.

**Notice they are different shapes.** Andel bought enormous attention and very little direct action. LetsGo bought a fifth of the attention and 554 clicks plus 343 comments.

Neither is wrong. They were built for different jobs, and that is the first decision you make.

---

## 1. Decide what the launch is for, before you write anything

Three jobs, and a post can really only do one well.

**Awareness.** You want the category to know you exist. Video, broad story, low friction, few asks. Andel's launch is this shape: 137 hours of watch time, 15 link clicks.

**Pipeline.** You want qualified people in a list. Text, specific offer, two clear actions. LetsGo's launch is this shape: 554 clicks, 343 comments.

**Credibility.** You want existing buyers to take you more seriously. Numbers, proof, named partners. Fewest impressions, highest quality.

Pick one. Grade the post against that one afterwards. A launch built for pipeline that gets judged on impressions will always look like a failure, and a launch built for awareness that gets judged on signups will too.

---

## 2. Build the amplifier list before you write the post

This is the step nobody copies and it is the one that decides the outcome.

You need roughly **50 close contacts** and **100 or more people you have met over the years**, plus customers and investors.

Not a list of everyone you know. A list of people who would genuinely be pleased for you, because the ask only works if the answer is obviously yes.

Build it in a spreadsheet with four columns: name, channel you will use, timezone, sent yes or no. It takes an afternoon. It is reusable for every launch after this one.

---

## 3. Run the five day sequence

Launch day is day five. The work is days one to four.

| Day | Who | Channel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Thursday** | Close contacts, around 50 | LinkedIn DM or WhatsApp, each followed by a calendar invite for launch hour |
| **Sunday** | Investors and network, timezone one | |
| **Monday** | Investors and network, timezone two | Email |
| **Monday** | Nudge back to the first fifty | Same thread as Thursday |
| **Tuesday, launch hour** | Everyone | The link drops at once |

**Send the calendar invite.** It sounds excessive. It does more for the outcome than anything else on this list, because it converts an intention into a notification on the morning it matters.

### The ask is the same three words every time

**Like, comment, save.**

Save is the one people skip, and it is the strongest signal of the three. Ask for it explicitly or you will not get it.

Add one line: forward this to anyone who would get value from it. Forwards are worth more than any of the three.

### What the messages actually say

Four rules, and they matter more than the wording.

1. **Tell them before the world knows.** The reason the ask lands is that you gave them something first: advance notice, and the sense of being inside it.
2. **Say exactly what you want, with a time on it.** Not "support the launch." A like, a one line comment and a save, in the first hour.
3. **Say how long it takes.** Two minutes. People agree to two minutes.
4. **Give them an exit.** "No pressure at all if Tuesday is a mess for you." It costs you nothing and it is why people answer honestly rather than ghosting.

For investors, lead with a real update before the ask: where the product is, what the early customers did, what you are launching with. The ask goes at the bottom, after the substance.

---

## 4. Build two funnels into the post, not one

This is the mechanic that made the LetsGo launch work, and it is the most copyable thing in this document.

The post did not say "sign up at the link."

It said: **comment below, and fill in the form on the site.** Two actions, on purpose.

> 554 people clicked the link.
> 343 people commented.

Those are two different funnels. Most of the qualified signups never came through the link at all.

**Why it works.** A comment is free, public and takes four seconds, so people actually do it. Every comment pushes the post further, which puts it in front of more people, who comment. The link is a private, higher friction action that only the already-convinced take.

Had the post only said "sign up here", it gets 554 clicks and nothing else. The comment gate roughly doubled the top of the funnel and cost nothing.

**Where the qualifying happens.** Not in the comment. The form asked for role, age and company, scored against who the product is for. That is how 1,000+ signups became a number anyone could trust rather than a vanity count.

---

## 5. Use video when the story carries it, not by default

Andel's launch video did **64,122 views and 137 hours of watch time.** It also produced 15 link clicks.

Video buys attention and reach. It does not buy action, because the viewer is not in a clicking posture.

Use it when the launch is about belief: a category, a vision, a founder's reason. Use text when the launch is about an action: a waitlist, a product, a date.

Cross-post it to X the same hour. Same asset, second room, no extra work.

---

## 6. The forty eight hours after are where the money is

The post is a list of people who raised their hand in public. Almost nobody works that list.

**Day one and two, do this:**

1. Pull everyone who commented, reacted or saved.
2. Filter to the ones who match your ICP. Role, company, seniority.
3. Message every one of them personally, referencing the specific thing they did.
4. Convert to a call, a demo or a purchase. Not a newsletter signup.

This is where a launch becomes revenue rather than a good day on a chart. The engagement list has a shelf life of about a week, and it is the warmest list you will ever have.

**Then post again on day eight.** LetsGo's follow up, the founder's own story of leaving a job with five kids and no plan B, did **27,624 impressions and another 284 link clicks** eight days after launch. A launch is a window, not a day.

---

## 7. What to measure, and what to ignore

Three weeks after his launch, the same founder posted something motivational.

> **72,031 impressions.** Nearly double the launch post.
> **7 new followers. No signups.**

The launch did 38,291 and filled a waitlist in a day.

Reach is not the scoreboard. It is just the easiest thing to count, which is why everyone counts it.

**Measure instead:**

| Metric | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Saves per 1,000 impressions** | The closest free signal to intent LinkedIn gives you. A like is politeness. A save is "I want to do this later" |
| **Profile views from the post** | Someone went looking for you. That is a buying motion |
| **Qualified signups, scored** | Not raw signups. Scored against your ICP, or the number means nothing |
| **Conversations opened in 48 hours** | The only number that becomes revenue |

---

## What this cannot do for you

The honest part, because the rest is not believable without it.

**It will not work without the list.** Both of these launches had a founder with an existing network who was willing to spend four days asking people personally. If you skip days one to four and just post, you get the reach your account already had.

**These are two launches, not a law.** One pharma marketplace with a funding announcement attached, one AI education product with a waitlist. Both B2B, both founder-led, both on LinkedIn. If you sell to consumers, treat this as a starting hypothesis.

**The numbers are reach and engagement, not attribution.** I can show you 554 clicks and 343 comments. I cannot show you a clean line from impression to signed contract, because LinkedIn does not give anyone that, and people who claim it are usually counting something else.

**It does not work twice in a row on the same list.** The amplifier ask spends goodwill. Use it for launches, not for Tuesdays.

---

## The one page version

1. Decide if the launch is for awareness, pipeline or credibility. Grade it on that only.
2. Build the amplifier list first. Fifty close contacts, a hundred wider.
3. Message them personally four days out. Calendar invite for launch hour.
4. Ask for like, comment, save. Save is the one that matters.
5. Put two actions in the post. A comment gate and a link.
6. Qualify on the form, not in the comments.
7. Video for belief, text for action. Cross-post to X.
8. Work the engagement list within 48 hours.
9. Post again on day eight.
10. Count saves, profile views and conversations. Not impressions.
