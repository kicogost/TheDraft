The executive swipe file

Twenty-five of the best performing posts we published for B2B founders and executives this year, with the real numbers attached and the pattern underneath each one. Including the two that bought enormous reach and returned nothing.

What this is

I'm a partner at RallyUp, where we write LinkedIn for B2B founders and executives. In 2026 we've driven 9.6 million impressions across 24 accounts at 16 companies, from more than 750 posts.

This is the part of that work worth stealing. Every post below is real, every number comes from the account's own analytics, and the accounts are anonymised because the posts belong to the people who published them, not to me.

What I've kept is the shape: the angle, why it worked, and the thing you can lift without copying anyone's words.

The honest promise. These patterns move numbers on accounts that already have something to say. None of them manufacture a point of view, and none of them will rescue a post about nothing. If a guide tells you a format is the reason something worked, it's selling you the easy half.

Read it in three buckets

Every post we ship gets filed before it's written. Top of funnel, middle, bottom. We run roughly 70 / 20 / 10, and the only rule that matters is that each bucket gets graded on a different number.

3. Hiring posts

We've hired our entire team through these. They reach the fewest people of any post type in this file and they outperform almost everything on the measures that aren't reach.

The angle. Opened on frustration rather than a job title. Four bullets on who'd be right, an honest line that the role isn't for everyone, and an application mechanic with a filter inside it: comment with something you've built, and he'd only reply to comments with a personal video attached.

Why it performed. 37 saves and 14 sends. A send is somebody forwarding the role to a specific person, which is the single most valuable action on LinkedIn and the one nobody optimises for. Nothing gets forwarded like a good job.

What this can't do for you

The honest part, because the rest isn't believable without it.

These are 25 posts from 24 accounts, not a law. B2B, founder and executive led, almost all on LinkedIn, mostly in tech, healthcare and finance. If you sell to consumers, treat everything here as a hypothesis.

The patterns don't manufacture a point of view. Every post in this file came from someone with something specific to say, usually something they'd earned the right to say. The format is the cheap half.

These are reach and engagement numbers, not attribution. I can show you 230 profile views and 37 saves. I can't draw a clean line from a save to a signed contract, because LinkedIn doesn't give that to anyone, and people who claim it are usually counting something else.

Account size changes everything. A 471-impression post on a small account and a 178,179-impression post on a large one are not comparable, and I haven't normalised for it. Read the rates, not the totals.

The one page version

  1. File every post as top, middle or bottom of funnel before you write it. Roughly 70 / 20 / 10.
  2. Grade each bucket on a different number. Impressions, then saves and profile views, then comments and applications.
  3. Write the intro post properly. It's the highest converting post you'll publish, and it only happens once.
  4. End the intro with what you'll write about next.
  5. Video for belief, text for action. Expect 137 hours of attention and almost no clicks from the first one.
  6. Put two actions in a launch: a comment and a link. They're separate funnels.
  7. Publish the internal artifact, with the uncomfortable section left in.
  8. Answer your critics in a post, not in the comments, and answer with counts.
  9. Put the filter inside the hiring ask. Ask for something that takes effort.
  10. Every launch, raise and role has a follow-up post made of what happened.
  11. Keep four-second posts in the bank for the weeks the real one isn't ready.
  12. Never let the post that went viral decide next quarter.
