25 posts · 24 accounts · 9.6M impressions

The executive swipe file

Twenty-five of the best performing posts we published for B2B founders and executives this year, each with its real numbers and the pattern underneath. Two of them bought enormous reach and returned nothing, and they're in here too.

What this is

I'm a partner at RallyUp, where we write LinkedIn for B2B founders and executives. In 2026 the accounts we run did 9.6 million impressions across 24 accounts at 16 companies, from more than 750 posts.

This document is the part of that work worth stealing. Every post below is real and every number comes from the account's own analytics. I've anonymised the accounts, because the posts belong to the people who published them.

What I've kept is the shape of each post, why it worked, and the part you can lift without borrowing anyone's words.

The honest promise. These patterns move numbers on accounts that already have something to say. They won't hand you a point of view or rescue a post about nothing. Any guide that credits a format for a post's success is selling you the easy half.

Read it in three buckets

We file every post before it's written: top of funnel, middle or bottom. The mix runs roughly 70 / 20 / 10, and each bucket gets graded on a different number. That's the only rule here that matters.

BucketBuilt forGraded on
TopReach: a personal story, a take on this week's news, humourImpressions
MiddleTrust: the breakdown, the analysis, what you learned doing it at volumeSaves, profile views
BottomAction: the role, the launch, the raise, the offerComments, applications, calls

Grade all three on impressions and you'll kill the two that are working.

Most executives treat the intro as a warm-up, so almost nobody plans it, and it's usually the highest converting post they'll ever publish.

We've hired our entire team through these. They reach fewer people than any other post type in this file and beat almost everything on the measures that aren't reach.

What this can't do for you

The honest part, because the rest isn't believable without it.

25 posts from 24 accounts make a sample, nothing more. They're B2B, founder and executive led, almost all on LinkedIn, mostly in tech, healthcare and finance. If you sell to consumers, treat everything here as a hypothesis.

The patterns won't hand you a point of view. Every post in this file came from someone with something specific to say, usually something they'd earned the right to say. Format is the cheap half.

These are reach and engagement numbers, with no attribution. I can show you 230 profile views and 37 saves, but I can't draw a clean line from a save to a signed contract. LinkedIn doesn't give that data to anyone, and people who claim it are usually counting something else.

Account size changes everything. A 471-impression post on a small account can't be compared with a 178,179-impression post on a large one, and I haven't normalised for size. Read the rates and ignore the totals.

The one page version

  1. File every post as top, middle or bottom of funnel before you write it. Roughly 70 / 20 / 10.
  2. Grade each bucket on a different number: impressions, then saves and profile views, then comments and applications.
  3. Write the intro post properly. It's the highest converting post you'll publish, and you only get one.
  4. End the intro with what you'll write about next.
  5. Video for belief, text for action. A video launch can buy 137 hours of attention and almost no clicks.
  6. Put two actions in a launch, a comment and a link, because they're separate funnels.
  7. Publish the internal artifact, with the uncomfortable section left in.
  8. Answer critics in a post, with counts, and leave the comment thread alone.
  9. Put the filter inside the hiring ask by asking for something that takes effort.
  10. Every launch, raise and role has a follow-up post made of what happened.
  11. Keep four-second posts in the bank for the weeks the real one isn't ready.
  12. Never let the post that went viral decide next quarter.
