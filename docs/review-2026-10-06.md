## The questions, with thresholds set in advance

1. **Does email 2 hold above 40% open?** Below that, the subject line is the problem, not the sequence.
2. **Does anyone click the $100 offer in email 4?** Zero clicks on a sample above 15 means the offer is in the wrong place, at the end of a ten day decay, or the wrong offer entirely.
3. **What is the completion rate?** It was 11% on three people, which is not a number.
4. **Does the open rate decay flatten or keep falling?** 72, 50, 50, 33 on tiny samples. A flattening curve is healthy, a continued slide is not.
5. **Did anyone reply?** Email 1 asks for "Got it", email 2 asks for their draft message. Only Francisco's inbox knows. Replies matter more than any percentage here, for deliverability and because they are the raw material for the next issue.
6. **Where did the 29 September post settle** once mature, on saves per 1,000 and on capture rate? Both were still climbing at capture, so those figures are high water marks of a moving number and will fall.

## The hypothesis this tests

Written before the answers are known.

**The only monetising email in the sequence sits at position four**, at the point where attention is lowest by definition. That is a structural choice, not a measured failure, and on 30 September the sample was three people. If email 4 lands under 40% open with no clicks on a real sample, the candidate change is to move the offer earlier, or to split it so the ask appears twice.

Do not act on this before the numbers are in.

## Deferred: the beehiiv community

Decided 30 September, not built.

A community with one job can survive at small scale. A community for connecting cannot. This one has an obvious single job and it is already running manually: email 2 of the DM sequence says "Hit reply with the draft. I will tell you honestly what is wrong with it."

That is the community. It is currently in his inbox, one to one, which is **better** at this size because every reply is private research about what to build and what to sell.

**The trigger to build it:** when he is getting more draft messages than he can personally answer in a week. That is when one to one stops scaling and a shared room becomes an upgrade rather than an empty room. Likely a few hundred subscribers away, not thirty two.

**If built:** one channel named to instruct, such as `post-your-draft`, not five empty ones. Newsletter post syncing on, so the room is never actually empty in a quiet week. Free, no tier gating.
