## What The Draft is

> Your guide to B2B social and content strategy. Written from more than 750 posts a year for B2B founders and execs.

A weekly letter on what actually works in B2B social and content, with the numbers attached, including when they are bad.

**It used to be about getting hired without applying.** That changed on 15 October 2026, for one reason that outranks every table in this repo: Francisco did not want to write it. A newsletter the author resents dies, usually just as it starts working. At 62 subscribers and three issues, it was the cheapest moment it would ever be to change.

The pivot also fixed the structural problem that blocked every product decision for a month. The old audience was frequently out of work, so charging them was both hard and distasteful. Marketing leaders and founders have budgets.

## Who it is for

Marketing leaders, founders and the people who execute content inside B2B companies.

Captured at signup as a required field (`role`), six options:

1. Founder or CEO
2. Head of marketing, VP or CMO
3. Marketing manager or specialist
4. Sales, GTM or partnerships
5. Agency owner, freelancer or consultant
6. Something else

Plus optional employer and an optional free-text "what have you tried that has not worked".

**Ghostwriters and copywriters are a real secondary audience.** They want to see how the work is actually done at volume. They do not buy sponsorships, so keep them served and do not optimise for them.

**Never say "I have driven 7.9M impressions."** That total includes a colleague's account and his own. "The accounts we run" is true, "I" is not. The clean personal number is 3.2M.

## The differentiation

Measured against five competitors across three agencies, 1,084 of their posts analysed. Full named detail in `private/competitive-landscape.md`.

**Do not compete on scale.** He is the smallest account in that set on followers, list size, client count and posts written. Any positioning that leads with size loses.

Three things the field does not do:

### 1. Name the client and attach the outcome

Some competitors name logos. Some state revenue. **None do both on the same engagement.** Francisco has written permission to do exactly that.

### 2. Publish the failures, including clients'

Across the competitor set, churn and failure run at 1 to 6% of posts, and never their own losses in detail. The Draft's format is the post that worked **and the one that died**.

### 3. Teach the owned audience, in public, from 62

The single biggest hole in the landscape. Every competitor sells LinkedIn; essentially none teach building a list that outlives it. One has a 6,000 person newsletter and has taught it once in a year.

This is the only subject where being small is the content rather than something to explain away.

**The format edge is the comment gate.** The field has largely abandoned it on taste, while one competitor's own data shows gated posts pulling roughly three times the comments. Francisco's best post used one, and the LetsGo launch proved the two-funnel mechanic: 554 link clicks against 343 comments, with most qualified signups never touching the link.

## Honest about scope

The promise covers LinkedIn, X, Instagram and newsletters. **The hard data is LinkedIn only.** Everything else is run in public as an experiment with the numbers shown either way.

Say this out loud rather than hiding it. It is already in welcome email 1. It converts the weakest point into the reason to subscribe, and it licenses the "growing a newsletter from 62" series that nothing else in the market can run.

## What is settled, and what is not

**Settled:** the subject, the audience, the proof bank, the magnets, the honest-scope contract, the tagline.

**Open:**

- **What gets sold.** Sponsorships are the stated near-term goal, which is why `role` and `company` are captured at signup. No product is validated.
- **Whether the aggregate impressions claim survives scrutiny.** 1M+ in launches is asserted from the author's own records; the verified launch figure is about 308,000.
- **The $40,000 LetsGo revenue chain.** Stated, not yet verified end to end.
- **Whether the operator, rather than the founder, is the better buyer.** The role field will answer this. Revisit at 50 answers.

## Carried over, still true

Three findings from the old positioning that survive the pivot intact:

1. **Artifacts beat commentary.** Posts containing a real artifact scored 9.73 saves per 1,000 impressions against roughly zero for commentary.
2. **Effortful asks return nothing.** 0 replies from 112 asks that required the reader to act first, against 3 from 24 on a two-word ask. See `offer-strategy.md` section 12.
3. **Reach is not the scoreboard.** A motivational post at 72,031 impressions produced 7 followers. A launch at 38,291 filled a waitlist.
