# The Application Loop

**Status: parked, 7 October 2026.** Written and built as a live page, then pulled before it shipped. Not on the site. The route, the nav entry and the sitemap line were all removed in the same commit that created this file, so nothing renders it.

## Why it exists

`CLAUDE.md` specs a `/manifesto` page and it has never had a body. This was the body: name the trap the reader is in, step by step, then show them the view from the side of the desk that reads the applications. The contrast is the thing Francisco has that nobody else writing to job seekers does.

Structure follows the "Manual Loop" section of Jacob Pegs' Modern Maker sales page, which is the one part of that page worth borrowing at this stage. Per the house rule in `CLAUDE.md`, structure and measurement are copied and language never is. Every word and every number below is Francisco's.

## The copy

**The Application Loop.**

If the only move you have left is sending another application, you are in it. Almost everyone who messages me is. It looks like effort, and it is five steps that feed each other, none of which is the step that works.

**1. You find the posting.**

It has been live for days before you see it. By the time you open the form, a hundred people have already sent something. I posted one role in September. 158 people came at me for it.

**2. You tailor the CV.**

You rewrite it around the keywords, because you have read that software reads it before a person does. That part is true. So you spend your afternoon writing for a filter.

**3. Nothing comes back.**

Not a rejection. Nothing. Silence is the only outcome that teaches you nothing, so you cannot correct for it. You do not know what you got wrong, so you cannot stop doing it.

**4. You decide the CV is the problem.**

New template. Different format. A fourth font. You give up a weekend improving the one artifact that was never going to decide this, because it is the only part of the process you control.

**5. You send more.**

Volume becomes the plan. One reader told me he sent more than 500 applications in a year out of work, got five interviews out of it, and no offers. Then you open the job board again on Monday, because what else is there.

**What it looks like from the other side.**

I have read this from the other side. Of the 158 who messaged me about that role, we interviewed four. Under 3%.

The other 154 were not worse at the job. They were all doing the same five things, which is the one thing that guarantees nobody can tell them apart.

Everything I write is about the way out: you build something for one company, and you send it to the person who owns the problem. That is the whole method, and [the message that got me hired](/resources/chief-of-staff-dm) is where it started.
