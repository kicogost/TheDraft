# Offer strategy

What to sell, at what price, and in what order. Researched 22 September 2026 against real market prices and Francisco's own numbers. Companion to `docs/positioning-review.md`, which establishes who the audience is.

## The decision

Ladder: **free magnet → $39 file → $75 async review → $250 call, offered only to people who have already bought something.**

Retire the open $100 call. It is not overpriced, the market for a session is $100 to $300, so it sits at the floor. It is the wrong shape: every sale costs an hour he does not have, at an effective rate of $60 to $80.

## 1. Sequence, and why this order

**Now: the $75 async review.** Buyer sends the message they are about to send. He returns a 5 to 8 minute video pulling it apart plus a rewrite, 48 hour turnaround, no call. Zero build. About 15 minutes a unit, so ten a month is 2.5 hours for $750, roughly $300 an hour, four times what the call pays.

The strategic reason it goes first is not the money. **Every review is primary research.** Ten reviews reveal which mistakes repeat, and those mistakes are the table of contents for the file. It pays him to do the research that writes the product.

**Weeks 4 to 8: the $39 file.** 15 to 25 real cold messages annotated line by line, his own included, sat next to ones that got nothing. Plus the follow-up arc and the research routine before the send. Roughly 20 hours to build, and he already owns the raw material.

**Month 9 at the earliest: a $150 live session**, capped at 20, only once the list clears 400. Below that it cannot fill.

## 2. Why the file, specifically

His single best asset is an artifact, not advice. The cold DM teardown is the only thing a genuine stranger has ever opted in for. The two highest-save posts are both about getting a decision maker's attention. The message he sent is recent, verifiable and his, so nobody else can publish it.

Read against the value equation, his current offer is strong on dream outcome and weak everywhere else. **Perceived likelihood is the weakest lever**: one proof point, and it is his own rather than a customer's. The file fixes it, because the buyer reads the message that actually worked instead of taking his word for it. The review fixes time delay, because the buyer gets the message fixed before they send it.

## 3. Price evidence

| Comparable | Price |
| --- | --- |
| Job Hunter Pipeline, LinkedIn outreach system for job seekers | $37 |
| The Job Search Email Playbook, Cultivated Culture | $37 |
| Cold DM Mastery, Gumroad | $49 |
| Justin Welsh, The LinkedIn Operating System | $150 |
| Single async resume review | $89 to $149 |
| MentorCruise, 30 minutes | $79 |
| Career coaching, per session | $100 to $300 |

An analysis of 146,271 Gumroad products puts the best converting band at **$30 to $49**. Pay what you want produces 8% more sales at a 65% lower average price, so it is a launch tactic, not a model, and it would signal these messages are cheap when their entire pitch is that one of them was worth a $100k job.

$39 at launch, $19 in pre-sale, path to $59 after 20 testimonials. Sixty day unconditional guarantee, matching the nearest competitor. Refund exposure on low-ticket digital runs 2% to 5%, and the guarantee lifts the weakest lever, so it pays for itself.

## 4. Economics at his size

Assumes followers grow 4.5 a day outside spikes, career content reaching about 14,750 impressions a month, and a **0.35% impression to email rate**. That last figure is an assumption, not a benchmark, and every number below hangs on it. Measure it and re-run.

| Model | Year one | His hours | Per hour | Compounds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $39 file | ~$7,300 | ~20 | ~$365 | Yes |
| $75 review | ~$7,500 | ~28 | ~$270 | No |
| $150 workshop | ~$6,000 | ~18 | ~$330 | Partly |
| $20 community | ~$4,000 | 100+ | ~$40 | No |
| $100 call | ~$2,400 | ~30 | ~$80 | No |

Running the file and the review together is roughly **$13,000 to $16,000 in year one for about 50 hours**. That is the realistic ceiling at this audience size, and it is a defensible number rather than a hopeful one.

## 5. Validation gates, set before building

- **Review:** offer it in the P.S. of three career posts. Three paid in two weeks means it works.
- **File:** post one full teardown publicly, capture emails against a single qualifying question, then pre-sell at $19. **Kill criterion: fewer than 5 pre-sales from the first 100 emails means do not build it.** Not at $19, not at $9. The answers to the qualifying question then say what to build instead.
- **Workshop:** announce a date and a cap. Eight seats in ten days, run it. Three, refund and cancel publicly.

## 6. What not to do

- **No paid community.** $600 a month at 30 members against a permanent weekly obligation and 10% to 20% monthly churn. It dies the first week the day job gets busy, loudly, in front of the people he needs to trust him. Worst trade available to a time-poor operator and the most attractive on a spreadsheet.
- **No $500 cohort course.** Needs 500 to 850 engaged subscribers to fill. He has single digits.
- **No paid ads.** Zero sales means no known conversion rate at any price. Validate organically first.
- **No done-for-you writing.** Unbounded time, competes with his employer, and drops him into a Fiverr fight at $25.
- **No paid newsletter.** Revisit at 5,000 subscribers, not before.
- **No third free lead magnet.** Two magnets and almost no subscribers is not a supply problem.
- **Do not cut the $100 call to get a first sale.** Change its shape or retire it.

## 7. The honest caveats

- The 0.35% capture assumption drives every revenue figure. If the real rate is 0.15%, halve everything.
- Willingness to pay is entirely unproven. Saves are intent, not money. The pre-sale is the first real test, and it is designed to cost a weekend rather than a month if the answer is no.
- Refund rate on this specific niche is an estimate from general low-ticket norms.
