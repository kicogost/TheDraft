# Distribution plan

Written 30 September 2026. Companion to `docs/positioning-review.md`, which establishes who the audience is, and `docs/offer-strategy.md`, which establishes what to sell them.

Positioning is settled. Capture is working. The remaining constraint is reach, and the cheapest reach available is commenting.

## The trap

The obvious move is to comment on the biggest LinkedIn names: Justin Welsh, Sahil Bloom, Amelia Sordell, Lara Acosta, Will McTighe.

**Their audiences are founders and creators who want to grow on LinkedIn.** That is RallyUp's market, not Francisco's. Twenty minutes a day there buys visibility in front of people who want to buy content services, which is the exact confusion removed from the feed in late September.

His buyer sits in the comment section of career and job search creators.

## Tier 1, the niche match

- **Erin McGoff**, AdviceWithErin. The most followed career advice creator, 7M+ across platforms, Forbes 30 Under 30, LinkedIn Top Voice. Gen Z and millennial career audience.
- **Hanna Goefft**, Hanna Gets Hired. 500k+, career strategy, future of work, personal branding.

Highest return per minute. Their comment sections contain the people who messaged him in the last week.

## Tier 2, already warm

Lara Acosta, Will McTighe, Aidan Tseng, Darren McKee, Alec Kremins. Comment here for the relationship, not the reach. Their audiences are wrong, but Lara and Will both messaged him unprompted, and that is worth keeping alive.

## Tier 3, the unfair advantage

**Recruiters and talent leaders posting about hiring.**

He is the only person in this niche to have been on both sides in the same year: he sent the cold message that got him hired, then posted a job and read 128 attempts at the same move. When a recruiter posts about what candidates get wrong, he can answer with something specific that no career coach can match.

Their comment sections are also full of job seekers.

## Build the feed from language, not follower counts

Named lists go stale. Search LinkedIn for the phrases the audience uses rather than the topic: "I've applied to 200 jobs", "open to work", "career change", "no one is replying". Follow whoever wrote that post, and whoever wrote the best comment under it. Keep 15 to 20.

## The standard

From his own lead magnet, section five: ten to twenty comments a day, about twenty minutes. Lead with your own experience, never with praise. Accounts in the top one percent leave well over a hundred replies a week on other people's posts; the average account leaves a third of that.

He currently answers inbound well. The entire gap is doing it outbound, before anyone knows who he is.
