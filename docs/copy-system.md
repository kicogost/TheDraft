# The Draft copy system

Every piece of copy for Francisco Gost runs through this: newsletter issues, automation emails, lead magnets, site copy, subject lines, LinkedIn posts, resource pages.

Read this before writing a word. The house rules in section 5 are absolute and fail CI.

Read `docs/voice-edits-log.md` as well. This file holds the rules he decided. That one holds the rules his edits reveal, and it is updated every time he changes a draft.

## 1. The method, after Sam Parr

Sam Parr built The Hustle to 1.5 million readers on a small number of habits. They are not tricks, they are constraints that force the writing to earn attention.

**Write like you talk.** Read every draft aloud. If you stumble, or if you would never say it to a person in a pub, rewrite it. Contractions are fine. Starting a sentence with "And" or "But" is fine.

**One idea per sentence.** Then stop. The full stop is the most underused tool in marketing writing.

**Specificity beats adjectives.** Never "a lot of impressions". Say 146,676. Never "a big company". Say the name, or say why you cannot. A number is the only thing in a paragraph that cannot be rewritten by someone with a better vocabulary.

**The first line has one job: get the second line read.** That is all. It does not need to summarise, set context or introduce anybody. Start in the middle.

**Cut the throat clearing.** "I wanted to share", "In today's issue", "As you may know". Delete. Find the first sentence that carries information and start there.

**Open a loop early, close it late.** Say something that demands an explanation, then make them read to get it. "That felt like the win. It was not." The reader cannot stop there.

**Story carries the lesson.** Nobody remembers advice. They remember that a founder replied in thirty minutes. Wrap every principle in something that actually happened, with a date, a name and a number.

**Use "you" more than "I".** The story is his. The stakes are theirs.

**Short paragraphs.** One to three lines. Most people read on a phone. White space is not wasted space, it is what makes the thing look readable before it is read.

**Apply the "so what" test to every line.** If a sentence does not change what the reader thinks, feels or does, cut it. Most first drafts lose a third.

**Be willing to say the blunt thing.** "Two of the four are a waste of your time" gets opened. "Four post types to consider" does not. But the body must then earn the claim, or it is bait.

## 2. Subject lines

Curiosity plus specificity. One or the other alone fails.

- Pure curiosity is bait: "You won't believe this". Nothing is promised, so nothing is delivered.
- Pure specificity is dull: "Four LinkedIn post types". True and ignorable.
- Together: "Only two of the four get you hired".

Rules:
- Under about 45 characters, so it survives a phone inbox.
- **The body must pay off the subject.** A subject that contradicts its own copy reads as a con. This has happened once in this project and had to be fixed.
- Lowercase openers are fine, ALL CAPS is not.
- No emoji in subject lines unless the issue is genuinely light.
- Preview text extends the subject, it never repeats it.

## 4. Formats

**Newsletter issue.** Every Tuesday issue follows the eight section format in section 4a. The main piece inside it ("The play") keeps the old essay shape: punchy opening line, the story, the lesson broken into numbered reasons, one action the reader can finish in an evening.

**Automation email.** One idea per email. Shorter than an issue, 150 to 350 words. Ends with a single instruction, not a menu. The first email in any sequence asks the reader to reply with two words, because a reply teaches the inbox provider he is a real person and keeps him out of spam.

**Lead magnet.** Hook, the story of why it exists, numbered sections, an honest promise that states the limits. The promise section is not optional: saying what the thing cannot do is what makes the rest believable.

**Site copy.** Short claim plus a full stop as headings, for example "My books." Sentences under 20 words. Prose measure never wider than 65 characters.

**The reply ask.** Every sequence entry point asks for a reply. It is a deliverability mechanism first and a conversation second. It also generates the next issue: issue one of The Draft exists because a reader replied.

## 4a. The weekly issue format

Decided 23 October 2026, modelled on the fixed skeleton of Tyler Denk's Big Desk Energy: the same sections every week, different stories inside them. The positioning is "How top 1% startups crush organic marketing". The edge every section draws on is the inside view: the LinkedIn accounts RallyUp runs for B2B founders and execs, with real numbers from Ordinal, and RallyUp itself, which Francisco helps run like a cofounder.

About 900 to 1,100 words, skimmable, the same order every week:

| # | Section | What goes in it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Sponsor | One slot near the top, written in his voice. Until a sponsor buys it, it promotes one of his own resources. |
| 1 | Cold open | Two to four lines, personal and a little funny. Where he is writing from, what happened this week. |
| 2 | The play | The main piece. One tactic, story or launch from the accounts we run: the story, then the numbers, then the play the reader can steal. |
| 3 | Banger vs flop | The best and worst post of the week from the accounts we write for at RallyUp, and the section says so. Big save count, impressions, the first line, why one worked and the other did not, and a link to each post on LinkedIn (skip the link when the account is anonymised). Ranked on saves per 1,000 impressions where possible, not raw reach. |
| 4 | Steal this | One hook, template or line the reader can copy and use today. |
| 5 | In the wild | Three posts or campaigns from other B2B brands doing organic well, one line each on why it works. |
| 6 | The scoreboard | Building in public with RallyUp's real numbers: revenue milestones, MRR and ARR, clients, team, plus The Draft's subscriber count and what he tested on his own LinkedIn. |
| 7 | Off the clock | Mallorca, jiu-jitsu, a song of the week. The bit readers mention when they reply. |
| 8 | Sign-off | One click poll (🔥 / 👍 / 😴), the reply ask, then "Stay awesome," and "Francisco". |

**Design, every issue.** The email opens with the spinning-logo masthead (`public/email/masthead-anim.gif`, from `assets/email/animated/masthead.mjs`), in a section set to show in email only. The web version opens instead with the issue's own title image, in a section set to show on the website only, built from the URL `https://franciscogost.com/email/issue?title=<subject>&date=<28 October 2026>` (upload it to beehiiv with save_image, and set it as the post thumbnail too). Each section is one bordered card with its animated header on top; the GIFs live in `public/email/sec-*-anim.gif` and are regenerated with `assets/email/animated/headers.mjs` then `frames-to-gif.py`. Turn off the beehiiv title and subtitle in the email header, since the title image carries them.

**The play rotates through four types** so the main piece never repeats its shape: a launch breakdown, a teardown of a public B2B brand's LinkedIn, an experiment run across several accounts, and a founder story.

**Client data rules.** Banger vs flop and The play run on client numbers. Name only the clients cleared in the proof bank. Everyone else is anonymised by stage and sector, for example "a Series A fintech founder, 38,291 impressions", and screenshots have names and faces blurred. RallyUp's own numbers are his to share; a client's invoice, contract value or name next to a payment never is.

**No competitor bashing.** Big Desk Energy does it. For The Draft it reads as punching at future clients and sponsors.

**Weekly inputs** are gathered by the Draft Scout agent (`.claude/agents/draft-scout.md`), which reads Slack, Gmail, Ordinal and beehiiv and hands back a fact sheet per section. Draft from that sheet, never from memory.

## 5. House rules, absolute

These come from CLAUDE.md and `pnpm lint` fails on the first two.

- **No em dashes.** Anywhere: copy, code comments, commit messages, docs. Use a comma, a full stop or a colon.
- **Banned words:** unlock, empower, elevate, solutions, leverage.
- **Sentence case everywhere.** Never all lowercase headings, never Title Case headings.
- **Never paste anyone else's sentences.** The site is modelled structurally on jayyanginspires.com. Structure and measurement are copied, language never is. Rewrite from the pattern with Francisco's facts.
- **Reader messages are anonymised.** Strip the employer, caption pull quotes "Confidential Reader", blur screenshots. Ask before quoting anyone directly.
- **Resource titles cap at 25 characters**, or the social card headline wraps to a line it cannot fit.

## 6. The pass before shipping

1. Read it aloud. Mark every stumble.
2. Delete the first paragraph. Is it better? Usually yes.
3. Every claim: is there a number, a name or a date behind it?
4. Does the subject line get paid off in the body?
5. Run the "so what" test line by line.
6. `pnpm lint` for em dashes and banned words.
7. Check present-tense facts that will age: his age, headcount, revenue, job title.
