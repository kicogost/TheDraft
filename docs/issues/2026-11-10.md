## 01 The play

### 147 saves vs 3

Two posts from accounts we run went out this week with almost the same reach.

An investor's essay on why VCs pay 2 to 10x more for the same numbers: **97,298 impressions, 147 saves.**

A product launch film: **86,644 impressions, 3 saves.**

Per view, the essay was saved 44 times more often.

The film wasn't bad. 27,852 people watched it, and a launch is supposed to be seen. But nobody saves an announcement. You save something you'll need again.

Here's what the essay did that the film didn't.

### 1. It explained a system the reader is stuck inside.

Teams at $2-4M ARR can't get a term sheet while pre-product teams announce billion-dollar rounds. The essay explained why: funds are betting on their next fund, and LPs judge them on markups.

If you're raising, that's a map you'll want again.

### 2. It used numbers people will repeat.

$40-60M vs $100-200M. 2 to 10x. A 50-80% lower price. Every one of them is something a reader could quote in a meeting the next day.

### 3. It admitted the writer is part of the problem.

"Every fund sits inside this, ours included."

One line, and the essay stops sounding like a pitch.

### 4. It left the reader with a question.

It ended on a question investors can ask about the funds they back. People saved it so they could come back to the question.

> **Do this week:** before you post, ask one thing. Will anyone need this again next month? If not, it's an announcement. That's fine, just judge it on reach, not saves.

## 02 Steal this

This week's format comes from Oren Blank, CEO of Walnut: 1,453 impressions and 10 saves, almost 7 per 1,000 views.

> "I keep a "podcast diet" on how great companies get built, and how GTM keeps changing. 5 podcasts I believe you should listen to:"

**✂️ COPY THIS**

1. **I keep a "[topic] diet" on [the thing you care about].**
2. **[N] [things] I believe you should [action]:**
3. A numbered list. For each one, the name, one honest line on why, and a link.
4. Honorable mentions for the ones that nearly made it.
5. End with "P.S. What's on yours?"

Nobody can use five podcasts in one sitting, so they save the post for later. "Diet" makes the list personal, and the P.S. turns the comments into more recommendations.
