## 🎯 The play: the second launch is the one that converts

In August, LetsGo launched on LinkedIn. 38,291 impressions, 554 link clicks, 343 comments, and a waitlist that filled in under 24 hours.

Last week the platform went live. The posts were tiny by comparison:

- The go-live post did 1,637 impressions.
- It also got 131 link clicks.

That is 8% of everyone who saw it clicking through. The August launch, the one everyone talks about, converted at 1.4%.

Same founder, same audience, five and a half times the click rate.

Here's what happened, and what you can steal.

**1. The first launch built the list. The second one cashed it.**
In August most of the 38,291 people had never heard of Moe or LetsGo. By October the people still seeing his posts were the ones who commented, saved and joined the waitlist. Smaller room, warmer room.

**2. The go-live post did not re-explain the product.**
Everyone in that room already knew what it was. So it skipped the pitch and said one thing: it's live, here's the link.

**3. Video carried the story, text carried the click.**
Moe's launch video the day before did 821 impressions, 58 comments and 9,520 seconds of watch time, and almost no clicks. The text post the next day did the clicking. Same split we saw with Andel in June: video for belief, text for action.

**4. He kept going.**
A third post on 22 October, about the founding coaches, did another 1,111 impressions and 10 clicks. A launch is a window, not a day.

**Do this week:** if you launched something in the last three months, write the "it's live" post. No backstory, one link, posted as text.

## 🔥 Banger vs flop

**The banger:** Eric Ong at Lightbank.

> "I've been a VC for over a decade. Founders keep asking what actually goes in a good update."

81,260 impressions. 462 saves. 164 new followers and 579 profile views.

Why it worked: it answered a question founders were already asking, and it was a template, so people saved it to use later. 5.7 saves for every 1,000 people who saw it.

**The flop:** Matt Firestone, from the RallyUp team. (He said I could. Legend.)

> "Am I the only loser on LinkedIn who flies economy?"

74,151 impressions. 209 comments. 3 saves.

Why it flopped: it's a great conversation and a useless post. Everyone had an opinion, nobody had a reason to come back to it.

Almost the same reach. 462 saves against 3.

Reach tells you how many people saw it. Saves tell you how many wanted it.
